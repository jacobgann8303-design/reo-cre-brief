# Microlearning curriculum

One concept per brief, taught in about 120 words, always tied to something in that day's actual
numbers or headlines. The tie is what makes it stick — an abstract definition of a cap rate is
forgettable, the same definition applied to a listing they just looked at is not.

Rotate through in order. Track position in the memory file so concepts don't repeat until the cycle
completes.

## Tier 1 — Foundations (first three weeks)

1. **Cap rate** — NOI ÷ price. Why a "5 cap" is expensive and an "8 cap" is cheap.
2. **NOI** — what's in it, what isn't. Why capital expenditures sit below the line.
3. **Price per square foot** — the number they compute in Block 6, and its limits.
4. **The four asset classes** — office, industrial, retail, multifamily, and why data centers broke the list.
5. **The 10-year Treasury and cap rate spread** — the mechanism that connects Block 2 to property values.
6. **Basis point** — and why professionals never say "0.25 percent."
7. **Vacancy vs. availability** — two numbers that sound identical and aren't.
8. **Absorption** — net vs. gross, and what negative absorption signals.
9. **Class A / B / C** — a relative grade, not an absolute one.
10. **Leasable vs. rentable vs. usable square feet** — where the load factor hides.

## Tier 2 — Deal mechanics

11. **Debt service coverage ratio** — the lender's first screen.
12. **Loan-to-value and loan-to-cost** — and why they diverge on development.
13. **The capital stack** — senior debt, mezzanine, preferred equity, common equity.
14. **Equity waterfall and promote** — how sponsors actually get paid.
15. **IRR vs. equity multiple** — why a great IRR can be a small check.
16. **Cash-on-cash return** — the number that pays your distributions.
17. **Going-in vs. exit cap rate** — the single most abused assumption in underwriting.
18. **Pro forma** — and healthy skepticism toward one.
19. **Offering memorandum** — what a broker is and isn't telling you.
20. **Due diligence period** — what actually happens in those 60 days.

## Tier 3 — Leases and operations

21. **Triple net (NNN) vs. gross vs. modified gross** — who pays for what.
22. **Tenant improvement allowance and leasing commissions** — the real cost of a signed lease.
23. **Free rent and effective rent** — why the headline rate lies.
24. **Escalations and CPI bumps.**
25. **Anchor tenant and co-tenancy clauses** — retail's hidden fragility.
26. **Lease rollover risk and the maturity wall.**
27. **Percentage rent** — the retail landlord's upside.
28. **Common area maintenance (CAM) reconciliation.**

## Tier 4 — Capital markets and structure

29. **CMBS** — what Trepp is actually reporting on.
30. **REIT structure** — the 90% distribution requirement and what it forces.
31. **FFO and AFFO** — why REITs don't report earnings like other companies.
32. **1031 exchange** — the tax rule that shapes an enormous share of transactions.
33. **Opportunity zones.**
34. **Sale-leaseback** — turning owned real estate into capital.
35. **Ground lease** — owning the building without owning the dirt.
36. **Development spread** — yield on cost vs. market cap rate, and where developer profit lives.
37. **Construction loan draw schedule.**
38. **Preferred equity vs. mezzanine debt** — a distinction that matters in a workout.

## Tier 5 — Market analysis and career

39. **Submarket** — and why "Dallas" is not a market.
40. **Comparable sales (comps)** — how to find them and how to adjust them.
41. **Replacement cost** — the ceiling on rent growth.
42. **Supply pipeline** — reading a construction chart.
43. **Migration and household formation** — the Sunbelt thesis in two variables.
44. **Broker vs. principal vs. advisor** — who is actually in the room.
45. **The CRE career ladder** — analyst, associate, VP, and what each does.
46. **ARGUS** — what it is and why job postings ask for it.

---

## How to teach one

Bad: "A cap rate is net operating income divided by purchase price."

Good: "That Lubbock retail strip you looked at this morning was asking $2.4M. If it throws off
$168K of net operating income after expenses, that's a 7.0 cap — divide NOI by price. Cap rates run
inverse to value: when the 10-year Treasury climbed today, buyers started demanding higher cap
rates, which means they'll pay less for the same income stream. That's the entire link between
Block 2 and Block 6."

Anchor it to their day. Name the actual number.
