## Today's concept: Cap rate

The **capitalization rate** (cap rate) answers one question: *what return does a property produce relative to its price, ignoring debt?*

The formula is simple:

> **Cap Rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Property Value**

Net Operating Income (NOI) is rent collected minus operating expenses — taxes, insurance, maintenance — but *before* any mortgage payment.

Here's why today's 10-year at **4.74%** matters. Cap rates and Treasury yields move together because investors compare them constantly. If a risk-free Treasury pays 4.74%, a Houston industrial property better offer a higher return to justify the extra risk of owning real estate — illiquidity, tenant problems, roof replacements.

Look at today's Texas headline: DFW and Houston led national industrial deliveries. High supply can push rents down, which compresses NOI, which *lowers* your cap rate at a given price — or forces the price down to restore it.

If a warehouse generates $500,000 in NOI and sells for $8,333,000, the cap rate is exactly 6.0%. A buyer paying $10,000,000 for the same income gets only a 5.0% cap rate — and with Treasuries at 4.74%, that spread of just 26 basis points is thin. That thinness is why lending is tight right now.
