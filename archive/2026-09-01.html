Today's concept: Vacancy vs. availability

1. The everyday version

Imagine your apartment building has 100 units. Ten of them have no tenant right now — empty, no lease, no one paying rent. Those are vacant. But there are also five units where the current tenant has signed a new lease somewhere else and is moving out next month — they're still physically occupying the unit and paying rent today, but you already know the space is coming onto the market soon. A property manager who only counts the ten empty ones is missing the five that are about to become a problem.

2. The real version

In commercial real estate — office buildings, warehouses, retail strips — vacancy means space that is empty *right now* and generating no rent. Availability is broader: it includes vacant space *plus* any space where a tenant has given notice, is subletting, or is otherwise known to be leaving soon. Availability is what a prospective tenant actually sees when they search for space.

Here's why this matters with today's numbers. Bisnow reported this morning that lab rental rates have fallen nearly 18% since 2023 to $64 per square foot. If you only counted vacant lab space, you might think the market is tighter than it is. But if a biotech company signed a big lease in 2021, realized they don't need the space, and quietly put 50,000 square feet on the sublease market — that's available, not vacant. The real supply is larger than vacancy alone would tell you.

A simple way to see the gap: if a 200,000 SF building has 20,000 SF sitting empty plus 15,000 SF a tenant is actively subleasing:

  • Vacancy rate: 20,000 ÷ 200,000 = 10.0%
  • Availability rate: 35,000 ÷ 200,000 = 17.5%

Same building. Very different pictures of market health.

3. Why a professional cares

A landlord quoting vacancy to a prospective tenant is telling a technically accurate but incomplete story — a tenant's broker will look at the availability rate to understand their real negotiating leverage. When availability is rising even as vacancy holds steady, that's an early warning sign that rents are about to soften, because more supply is coming whether or not it shows up in the vacancy count yet.

Check yourself

A 500,000 SF office building reports a vacancy rate of 8%. You later learn that two tenants — together occupying 60,000 SF — have publicly announced they're relocating when their leases expire in four months. What is the building's current availability rate, and what does the gap between the two numbers tell a prospective tenant about their negotiating position?

Show answer

Vacant space is 8% × 500,000 = 40,000 SF; add the 60,000 SF of announced departures, and available space is 100,000 SF; 100,000 ÷ 500,000 = 20% availability rate. The gap — 8% vacant but 20% available — tells the tenant that a lot more supply is coming soon, which means they have strong negotiating leverage on rent and concessions even though the building looks mostly full today.

Review

Cap rate (Brief #1): Without looking it up — if you know a building's NOI (its annual income after operating expenses, before debt payments) and you know what price someone paid for it, how do you calculate the cap rate? And if two similar buildings sell at a 5 cap and an 8 cap, which one did the buyer pay more for relative to what it earns — and why might they accept that?

Show answer

Cap rate = NOI ÷ purchase price; the 5 cap buyer paid more relative to income (e.g., $100,000 NOI ÷ 5% = $2,000,000 price vs. $100,000 ÷ 8% = $1,250,000), meaning they accepted a smaller return — typically because they expect the property to be lower risk or located in a stronger market where they're confident the income will hold.

NOI (Brief #2): Name two things that ARE included in the calculation of NOI and one thing that is NOT — and say in one sentence why that excluded item sits below the line rather than inside NOI.

Show answer

NOI includes rental income and is reduced by operating expenses like property taxes and insurance; debt service (mortgage payments) is excluded because NOI is designed to measure the property's performance independently of how it was financed, so two investors with different loan terms can compare properties on equal footing.
